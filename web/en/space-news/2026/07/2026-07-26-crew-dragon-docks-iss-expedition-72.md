# Crew Dragon Docks with ISS, Begins Expedition 72 Operations

**Summary:** The Crew Dragon spacecraft autonomously docked with the International Space Station on July 26, 2026, marking the start of Expedition 72 research activities.

On July 26, 2026, SpaceX's Crew Dragon spacecraft successfully completed an autonomous docking with the International Space Station, officially commencing Expedition 72's new cycle of on-orbit residency and research operations. According to reports, the spacecraft followed standard rendezvous procedures as it gradually approached the station, with the entire process managed by the vehicle's automated flight control system under continuous monitoring from ground-based mission control centers. The exact crew size and crew member identities await further confirmation from NASA and SpaceX.

This mission is part of NASA's Commercial Crew Program, which leverages commercial spacecraft to provide regular crew rotation and cargo resupply services to the International Space Station. Since entering service, the Crew Dragon has successfully completed multiple crewed flight missions, accumulating extensive experience in on-orbit docking operations. Following the docking, the newly arrived astronauts are expected to conduct handover procedures with the crew already aboard the station before formally beginning their scientific research activities.

Expedition 72's on-orbit work encompasses multiple scientific disciplines, reportedly including physics experiments in microgravity environments, life sciences research, and Earth observation projects. The International Space Station continues to serve as a critical platform for long-duration human spaceflight, providing unique experimental conditions for research institutions worldwide. The successful completion of this docking mission further validates the reliability of commercial crew transportation systems and lays the groundwork for subsequent space station operations.
