# JAXAs Latest Rocket Launch Carries Science Experiments

**Summary:** On July 26, 2026, JAXA launched an H3 rocket carrying multiple science experiments for the International Space Station.

On July 26, 2026, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) successfully launched an H3 rocket from the Tanegashima Space Center in Kagoshima Prefecture. The mission, one of the latest orbital flights in the H3 rocket series, carried cargo bound for the International Space Station, including multiple science experiment payloads. According to information released by JAXA, the launch proceeded smoothly, with the rocket entering its planned orbit as scheduled.

The H3 rocket is JAXA's next-generation flagship launch vehicle, developed to replace the retired H-IIA series and manufactured by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries. After a failed maiden launch in March 2023, the H3 successfully completed its demonstration flight in 2024 and has since transitioned into routine operations. The rocket was designed to enhance Japan's space launch capabilities while significantly reducing launch costs and strengthening competitiveness in the international commercial launch market.

The science experiment payloads carried on this mission reportedly span multiple research fields and will support microgravity science experiments conducted aboard the International Space Station. As a key partner in the ISS program, Japan has long supplied the station with cargo and experiment equipment through its HTV cargo spacecraft and commercial resupply missions. The detailed manifest of experiments and specific research objectives for this mission are expected to be disclosed in subsequent announcements from JAXA.
