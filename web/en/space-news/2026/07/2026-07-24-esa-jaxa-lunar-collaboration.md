# ESA and JAXA Sign Agreement for Lunar Exploration Collaboration

**Summary:** ESA and JAXA have signed a new agreement to collaborate on lunar exploration missions, focusing on science and technology exchange. Date 2026-07-24.

On July 24, 2026, the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) formally signed a new agreement to strengthen cooperation in lunar exploration. The agreement focuses on science and technology exchange, with plans to share data, technical resources, and research outcomes in future lunar missions to advance exploration efforts.

According to reports, this collaboration covers several key technical areas, including lunar lander design, spacecraft technology, and scientific payload development. Both agencies intend to enhance their capabilities for operations on the lunar surface through joint research and personnel exchanges, laying the groundwork for future crewed or uncrewed missions. Specific cooperation details and timelines remain to be confirmed by the agencies.

ESA and JAXA are both committed to sustainable lunar exploration. ESA is advancing initiatives like the Moon Village concept and the European Large Logistics Lander (EL3), while JAXA is involved in international lunar exploration frameworks and developing its own landers. This agreement aims to leverage the strengths of both parties to accelerate the construction of lunar research stations and resource utilization.

This collaboration marks a new milestone in international space cooperation, helping to reduce costs and increase mission success rates for lunar exploration. In the future, the partnership may expand to broader space exploration domains, jointly addressing the challenges of deep space.
