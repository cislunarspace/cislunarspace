# Scientists Create Quantum Matter Waves on International Space Station

**Summary:** As reported on July 31, 2026, NASA announced upgrades to the ISS quantum laboratory, leveraging microgravity to probe ultracold atoms and successfully create quantum matter waves.

According to a report from China Economic Net, NASA announced on July 31, 2026, that its quantum laboratory aboard the International Space Station (ISS) has undergone its latest round of technological upgrades. The primary objective of this upgrade is to leverage the unique microgravity environment of the space station to conduct unprecedented precision probing and manipulation of ultracold atoms.

In the upgraded experiments, scientists successfully created quantum matter waves. Matter waves are a fundamental concept in quantum mechanics, indicating that all matter particles exhibit wave-like properties. In ground-based laboratories, gravity severely disrupts the observation of ultracold atoms. The near-zero gravity environment provided by the ISS allows scientists to maintain and observe these quantum-state atomic clouds for much longer periods and with far greater precision, leading to the successful generation and clear observation of quantum matter waves.

This breakthrough lays the groundwork for conducting more cutting-edge quantum physics experiments in space in the future. According to NASA, studying quantum matter waves on a microgravity platform is expected to drive technological advancements in fields such as quantum sensing, quantum navigation, and even the precision measurement of fundamental physical constants. Specific technical details and subsequent research plans for this experiment await further disclosure from the relevant institutions.
