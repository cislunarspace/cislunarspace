# Blue Origin's New Glenn Rocket Explodes During Prelaunch Test, Fourth Flight Indefinitely Postponed

**Summary:** On May 28, 2026, Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket exploded during a routine prelaunch hotfire engine test at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station's Launch Complex 36 (LC-36) in Florida. The massive fireball lit up the night skies over the Space Coast, damaging both the rocket and critical launch infrastructure. No injuries were reported, but the accident threatens to ground the 320-foot-tall (98 m) rocket for an extended period. The planned June 4 mission — New Glenn's fourth flight, slated to deploy 49 Amazon Kuiper broadband satellites — has been indefinitely postponed.

## What Happened

Blue Origin confirmed the explosion in a post on social media platform X on Thursday night (May 28): "No injuries. We are conducting a full assessment." Multiple rocket-watchers captured the explosion on video, showing a huge fireball erupting at LC-36, the only pad currently supporting New Glenn launches.

The accident occurred during a routine hotfire test — a standard prelaunch procedure in which the rocket's engines are briefly ignited while still on the pad to verify all systems are functioning properly before liftoff.

## Impact on NASA's Moon Plans

The New Glenn explosion carries significant implications for both Blue Origin and NASA. Blue Origin's rocket is the chosen launch vehicle for the company's Blue Moon lunar lander, one of two private vehicles (alongside SpaceX's Starship) selected by NASA to ferry astronauts to the lunar surface under the Artemis program.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman addressed the explosion on X: "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult. We will work with our partners to support a thorough investigation of this anomaly, assess near-term mission impacts, and get back to launching rockets."

Earlier this week, NASA announced that Blue Moon would land two private lunar rovers on the moon — key stepping stones in the agency's planned Artemis base buildup near the lunar south pole. A robotic prototype, Blue Moon Mark 1, was also slated to attempt its first lunar landing later this year.

## Damage to the Launch Pad

LC-36 is currently the only pad capable of supporting New Glenn launches. Video evidence suggests extensive damage to the launch infrastructure, which could keep New Glenn grounded for months while repairs are assessed and carried out.

## New Glenn Flight History

New Glenn has flown only three times to date:

- **NG-1** (January 2025): Maiden flight — success, Blue Ring platform delivered to orbit, booster successfully recovered.
- **NG-2** (April 2025): Success, multiple satellites deployed, booster recovered.
- **NG-3** (April 19, 2026): Second stage malfunction failed to deliver AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird 7 satellite to its intended orbit; satellite will reenter and burn up in Earth's atmosphere. The FAA briefly grounded the vehicle pending investigation.

The May 28 explosion marks New Glenn's second failure in just over a month.
