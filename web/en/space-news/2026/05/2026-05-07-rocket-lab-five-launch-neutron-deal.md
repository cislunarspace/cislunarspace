# Rocket Lab Signs Five-Launch Neutron Deal, Confirms Late 2026 Debut Target

**Summary:** Rocket Lab announced a block sale of five Neutron rocket launches alongside three additional Electron flights during its Q1 2026 earnings call on May 7, while reaffirming its commitment to the Neutron's debut flight in late 2026.

## Contract Details

The company disclosed that the deal — covering five Neutron launches and three Electron missions — surpassed its previous record, a $190 million contract for 20 hypersonic suborbital test flights of the Haste version of its Electron rocket for the Department of Defense. Rocket Lab did not reveal the monetary value of the new contract.

During the earnings call, CFO Adam Spice said the company ended the quarter with approximately $2.2 billion in backlog, with launches accounting for 41.5 percent of that figure.

> "We are actively cultivating a strong pipeline that includes multi-launch agreements, large satellite platform contracts, and an increasingly diverse set of satellite component and subsystem merchant opportunities across government and commercial programs," Spice said. "These larger, needle-moving opportunities can introduce lumpiness and backlog growth, but they are critical drivers of long-term value and scale for the business."

## First Flight Progress

CEO Peter Beck told investors to watch for "placing of items on test stands" as the benchmark of progress toward Neutron's first launch, currently targeted for Q4 2026 from Launch Complex 3 at the Mid-Atlantic Regional Spaceport within NASA's Wallops Flight Facility in Virginia.

He said the team has made "tremendous strides" on the stage one tank design following an unintended rupture during a test earlier this year, improving both tank strength margins and manufacturability. "We've cleared separation events at full flight loads on the second stage article and interstage development system," Beck added. "If you see something broken on the test stand from here on, know that's completely intentional."

## Engine Testing

The Archimedes engine — nine liquid methane-fueled engines producing nearly 1.5 million pounds of thrust at liftoff — is undergoing intensive testing at NASA's Stennis Space Center. Beck reported full-duration burns with thrust vector control across the full conical sweep, vacuum engine hot fires, and both Archimedes test cells in operation simultaneously.
