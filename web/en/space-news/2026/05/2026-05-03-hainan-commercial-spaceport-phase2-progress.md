# Hainan Commercial Spaceport Phase II Surges Ahead: No. 3 Launch Pad 80%+ Complete, Targeting 60 Launches Per Year

**Summary:** According to a CCTV Space Window report on May 3, 2026, the Hainan Commercial Spaceport's Phase II construction is in full acceleration. The No. 3 launch pad has surpassed 80% completion, the lightning towers have reached their eighth section, and two auxiliary buildings have fully topped out with interior finishing underway. When all four pads are operational, the facility's annual launch capacity will reach 60 missions, establishing the port as China's core hub for high-frequency commercial spaceflight.

## Phase II Construction Achieves Multiple Milestones

The Hainan Commercial Spaceport, China's first dedicated commercial launch facility, is rapidly advancing its Phase II expansion with No. 3 and No. 4 launch pads. The No. 3 pad has exceeded 80% completion, entering its final construction sprint.

The lightning towers—a critical safety infrastructure for launch facilities—have completed the eighth section, continuing to rise. Two auxiliary equipment buildings have fully topped out, with interior work and partial equipment installation already underway, laying a solid foundation for on-schedule completion.

## Annual Capacity to Reach 60 Launches: China's Commercial Spaceflight Hub

Positioned as China's core hub for high-frequency, commercial spaceflight operations, the Hainan spaceport currently operates Pads 1 and 2. Construction of Pads 3 and 4 is accelerating, with all four pads expected to be operational in the near future. Once complete, the facility will support up to 60 launches per year, serving diverse domestic and international commercial launch needs.

Unlike national team launch sites, the Hainan Commercial Spaceport specializes in commercial missions, adopting an agile "launch-on-demand" model targeting one launch per week.

## Strategic Significance: Enabling China's Spacepower Ambitions

As China's first commercial space launch facility, the Hainan Commercial Spaceport holds strategic importance for the nation's spacepower goals. The facility achieves full domestication of systems and equipment, ensuring technology self-sufficiency and providing a sovereign launch guarantee for China's commercial space industry.

The port's geographic advantage—located at a low latitude—allows rockets to leverage Earth's rotational velocity, reducing propellant consumption and boosting payload capacity, delivering a meaningful launch efficiency edge.
