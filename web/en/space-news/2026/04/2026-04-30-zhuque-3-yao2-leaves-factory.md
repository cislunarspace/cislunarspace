# LandSpace Zhuque-3 Yao-2 Rocket Enters Factory Departure Phase, Targeting Q2 Launch

**Summary:** In April 2026, LandSpace's Zhuque-3 Yao-2 reusable liquid methane rocket completed all problem resolution work and officially entered the factory departure phase, en route to the launch site. The rocket targets a first-stage recovery test during Q2, a critical step in verifying the Zhuque-3's reusability technology. Following the Yao-1 mission's recovery anomaly, Yao-2 will incorporate technical improvements for its reflight verification.

![Zhuque-3 Yao-2 rocket imagery](./figures/2026-04-30-zhuque-3-yao2-leaves-factory/hero.jpg)
*Image credit: LandSpace*

Zhuque-3 is a reusable liquid methane carrier rocket independently developed by LandSpace, featuring a 4.2-meter diameter and an all-stainless steel structure, making it China's first stainless steel launch vehicle. The first stage is equipped with 9 Tianque TQ-12B liquid methane engines, while the second stage uses a single TQ-15B vacuum engine. The rocket delivers 21.3 tonnes to low Earth orbit on expendable missions, 18.3 tonnes for return-to-launch-site recovery operations, and 12.5 tonnes for return-to-launch-site with return flight, with a target of over 20 reuses.

On December 3, 2025, Zhuque-3 Yao-1 achieved a successful orbital insertion, but during the first-stage vertical recovery test, an anomaly occurred during the landing reignition sequence, causing the booster to land at the edge of the recovery pad rather than achieve a soft landing. LandSpace's technical team completed comprehensive problem resolution for the issues exposed during the Yao-1 flight, ensuring Yao-2 returns to the launch site in a more reliable condition.

According to plan, Zhuque-3 Yao-2 will execute a full-orbital first-stage recovery test during Q2, verifying the key technologies for vertical takeoff and landing. If the Yao-2 recovery test succeeds, LandSpace aims to attempt its first re-flight with a recovered booster during Q4 — meaning the first stage will be recovered, inspected, and re-launched, achieving true reusability.

LandSpace founder and CEO Zhang Changwu has stated that the Zhuque-3's reusability technology will significantly reduce launch costs for commercial spaceflight, with launch prices expected to drop from the current approximately 60,000 RMB per kilogram to below 20,000 RMB per kilogram, effectively supporting national satellite internet projects and major space infrastructure construction.
