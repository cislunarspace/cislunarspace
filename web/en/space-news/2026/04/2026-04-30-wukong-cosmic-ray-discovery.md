# DAMPE Satellite Discovers Cosmic Ray 'Bump' Structure, Revealing Charge-Dependent Acceleration Limit

**Summary:** China's Dark Matter Particle Explorer (DAMPE, also known as 'Wukong') satellite has been operating smoothly in orbit for over 10 years since its launch in late 2015. Using 9 years of observational data (2016–2024), an international research team led by the Purple Mountain Astronomical Observatory of the Chinese Academy of Sciences has precisely measured the energy spectra of five cosmic ray species: protons, helium, carbon, oxygen, and iron. For the first time, they directly observed a consistent 'bump' structure — a sharp decrease in particle numbers at a specific high-energy threshold — across all five species. Crucially, the team found that it is the particle's electric charge, not its mass, that determines this acceleration limit. The findings, published online in Nature on April 29, 2026, provide the first direct observational evidence for a charge-dependent cosmic ray acceleration limit.
