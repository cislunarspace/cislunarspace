# China Launches Tianlian 2-05 Data Relay Satellite, Boosting Space-based TT&C Capabilities

**Summary:** At 15:54 UTC on April 27, 2026 (23:54 Beijing Time), China successfully launched the Tianlian 2-05 geostationary data relay satellite using a Long March 3B carrier rocket from the Xichang Satellite Launch Center. The satellite entered its predetermined orbit, marking a complete success. The Tianlian 2-05 is the fifth satellite in China's second-generation geostationary data relay satellite system and will operate alongside Tianlian 2-04 (launched in March 2026) to further enhance China's space-based tracking, telemetry, and command (TT&C) capabilities. This launch was the 572nd flight of the Long March rocket family.

![Long March 3B carrying Tianlian 2-05 lifts off from Xichang (Xinhua photo)](./figures/2026-04-27-tianlian-2-05-launch/hero.jpg)
*Credit: Xinhua (Photo by Yang Xi)*

The Tianlian 2-05 satellite was developed by the China Academy of Space Technology (CAST) under CASC. As the newest member of China's second-generation geostationary data relay satellite constellation, it serves as a critical "space WiFi" and "super messenger" in orbit. Its primary missions include providing data relay and TT&C services for crewed spacecraft such as Shenzhou spaceships and China's Tiangong space station, relaying data from medium and low Earth orbit satellites, and supporting the TT&C of launch missions.

The launch of Tianlian 2-05 comes just one month after the successful launch of Tianlian 2-04 on March 26, 2026. Once operational, the two satellites will work in concert to significantly expand the coverage and data transmission capacity of China's space-based TT&C infrastructure, providing more reliable space-based information support for crewed spaceflight and other major missions.

China's data relay satellite program has grown from strength to strength. Following the initial development launch in 2003, the first Tianlian-1 satellite was sent to orbit in 2008, and by 2012 a three-satellite constellation of Tianlian-1 spacecraft was established, making China the world's second country — after the United States — to possess a globally capable data relay satellite system. Today, the second-generation Tianlian-2 series continues to extend and improve this capability.

The Long March 3B rocket used for this launch was developed by the China Academy of Launch Vehicle Technology (CALVT) under CASC. As a flagship model of the Long March 3A rocket family, the Long March 3B is China's first single rocket variant to surpass 100 orbital launch missions, renowned for its reliability. This successful launch further demonstrated the rocket's dependability and marked the 572nd flight in the Long March series' distinguished history.
