# NASA Reveals Artemis 3 Crew

**Summary:** NASA officially announced the four-person Artemis 3 crew on June 9, 2026, at the Johnson Space Center in Houston. Commander Randy "Komrade" Bresnik (58, former Marine fighter pilot and TOPGUN graduate), ESA pilot Luca Parmitano (49, two long-duration ISS stays), mission specialist Andre Douglas (40, Artemis 2 backup crew member), and mission specialist Frank Rubio (49, holder of the U.S. record for 371 consecutive days in space) were selected.

## Mission Profile: Earth-Orbit Validation First

Artemis 3 is targeted for mid-to-late 2027, but contrary to public expectations of a direct lunar landing, the mission's primary objective is to validate rendezvous and docking procedures with SpaceX's Starship and Blue Origin's Blue Moon Mark 2 landers in Earth orbit. The crew will launch aboard the SLS rocket and Orion spacecraft, then chase and dock with lunar landers one at a time in orbit, confirming that hardware interfaces, software systems, propulsion, and life-support elements can operate together in the high-stakes space environment.

Jeremy Parsons, a senior manager in NASA's Moon to Mars program office, said: "This test flight will enable us to prove we can carry out highly choreographed operations with our partners across multiple launch pads and meet up at precise points in space."

Artemis 2 commander Reid Wiseman passed a symbolic baton to Bresnik at the ceremony — linking the Artemis 2 lunar flyby to the Artemis 3 orbital validation and on to Artemis IV's actual surface landing.

## The Four-Person Crew

**Commander Randy "Komrade" Bresnik** — Age 58, former Marine fighter pilot and TOPGUN graduate. First flew on the Space Shuttle in 2009, followed by a long-duration ISS stay in 2017, accumulating 149 days in space. At the ceremony, Bresnik said: "We are doing flight tests on every single flight, incrementally determining the flight envelope, expanding it, proving out capabilities and making the operational procedures that we have more and more precise. Because every single mission we will do after this will be more challenging and more complex."

**Pilot Luca Parmitano** — Age 49, Italian ESA astronaut with two long-duration ISS stays. He represents the international partnership in the Artemis program. ESA builds the Orion service module, making it NASA's most significant international Artemis partner.

**Mission Specialist Andre Douglas** — Age 40, space rookie who served as the Artemis 2 backup crew member, training alongside the prime crew through launch day. He previously worked at the Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory on planetary defense and robotic exploration missions.

**Mission Specialist Frank Rubio** — Age 49, spent a U.S.-record 371 consecutive days aboard the ISS in 2022-23. Former Army helicopter pilot and flight surgeon.

## Context: Lander Competition and Time Pressure

The orbital-validation approach reflects the different challenges facing the two HLS contractors. Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket exploded during a static-fire test at Cape Canaveral's LC-36 on May 28, 2026, causing major pad damage. The company expects to return to flight before year's end, but the Blue Moon Mark 1 uncrewed cargo lander (originally planned for fall 2026) has been delayed, and it remains uncertain whether the larger Mark 2 crewed lander will be ready in time for Artemis 3.

SpaceX continues refining the Super Heavy-Starship system and is equipping a Starship upper stage with a docking mechanism for Artemis 3, though this version is not yet a full operational lander. The timeline for a flight-ready landing version remains unclear.

NASA aims to return astronauts to the lunar surface by the end of 2028 — ahead of China's goal of landing taikonauts on the Moon by decade's end. Following Artemis IV and V, NASA plans two crewed landings per year, with construction of a lunar south pole base beginning around 2029-2030.
