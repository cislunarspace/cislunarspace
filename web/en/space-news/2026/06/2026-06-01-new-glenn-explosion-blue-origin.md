# Blue Origin's New Glenn Rocket Explodes: Hours After Receiving U.S. Military Launch Contract

**Summary:** On the evening of May 28, 2026, Blue Origin's New Glenn heavy-lift rocket exploded during a static fire test at its Florida launch pad, severely damaging the facility. Just hours earlier, the U.S. Space Force had awarded the company a national security launch contract — a dramatic coincidence that drew widespread attention.

## Incident Overview

On the evening of May 28, Blue Origin's New Glenn heavy rocket exploded during a static fire test at its Florida launch pad. The scene erupted into a massive fireball, engulfing the entire first-stage booster. The shockwave was so powerful it could be heard clearly from miles away.

Witnesses described the massive fireball as visible from four miles away. Debris scattered across several miles, and the launch pad facility sustained severe damage.

Fortunately, there were no casualties reported.

## Launch Facility Severely Damaged

More critically, New Glenn's only operational launch pad was devastated in the explosion. Estimates suggest repairs could take at least six months, with costs comparable to building a completely new launch facility.

This means Blue Origin's launch capabilities will be severely limited for the foreseeable future.

## Dramatic Timing: Contract Awarded Hours Before Explosion

Just hours before the explosion, the U.S. Space Force announced on May 29 that it had awarded Blue Origin a national security launch contract. This dramatic timing drew widespread attention to the incident.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman conducted an aerial survey of the Blue Origin launch facility on May 29 following the New Glenn anomaly.

## Impact on NASA's Lunar Return Program

Blue Origin had planned to use the New Glenn rocket later this year for a lunar mission, delivering scientific equipment to the lunar south pole and validating key technologies for future crewed landings.

This explosion has temporarily deprived NASA of a key partner in its lunar base program. The company's rocket design will require reassessment, which could significantly impact Blue Origin's subsequent launch plans and add new uncertainty to related lunar missions.

According to Nature, Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket was deeply involved in executing NASA's Artemis program, and this explosion adds new complications to America's return-to-moon efforts.

## Market Reaction

The Blue Origin explosion caused aerospace stocks to fall broadly, with partner AST SpaceMobile's stock dropping nearly 15%. The U.S. commercial space sector as a whole came under pressure.
