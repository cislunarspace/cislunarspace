# A Cold War 250 Miles Up: ISS Air Leak Exposes US-Russia Mistrust, Astronauts Shelter in Dragon

**Summary:** On June 20, 2026, a sudden worsening of the Zvezda module air leak forced NASA astronauts to shelter inside a docked SpaceX Crew Dragon, after Roscosmos unilaterally began repairs — exposing deep institutional mistrust between the two agencies.

The International Space Station has a long-running air leak problem, and the Zvezda service module has been the primary source. Russian crews have attempted multiple repairs over the years with limited success. On June 20, the leak rate in Zvezda suddenly worsened to a level that threatened crew safety. According to El País, NASA ordered the station's astronauts to move into the SpaceX Crew Dragon capsule docked at the station, keeping it on standby as a lifeboat — an extraordinary emergency measure rarely invoked in the station's more than two decades of continuous habitation.

Compounding the crisis was Roscosmos's response. The Russian space agency reportedly began repair work on Zvezda unilaterally, without sufficient technical coordination with its American counterparts. The move caught NASA off guard and directly prompted the decision to shelter the crew inside Dragon. Tensions over information sharing around the leak have been simmering for some time; this incident brought the two agencies' deep institutional mistrust into the open.

Since its first modules launched in 1998, the ISS has stood as the flagship project of US-Russia space cooperation. Even as geopolitical relations deteriorated sharply, both sides maintained a working technical partnership aboard the station. The Zvezda leak episode suggests that this cooperative framework is under growing strain. As El País noted, unless the two agencies can rebuild basic trust around station safety and operations, the risks to the ISS during its remaining operational life will only increase.
