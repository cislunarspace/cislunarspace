# NASA to First Use Private Spacecraft to Service Government Satellite; Swift Observatory as Testbed

**Summary:** NASA has announced that Link, a private spacecraft built by Arizona-based Katalyst Space Technologies, will launch in late June 2026 to rendezvous with and boost the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory — the first-ever attempt by a commercial entity to capture and service an operational U.S. government satellite in orbit.

## The Link Mission: From Concept to Implementation

NASA announced on May 28 that Link, a private spacecraft, will launch in late June 2026 to dock with the 20-year-old Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory. Swift was launched into low Earth orbit in 2004 primarily to observe gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) and other high-energy astrophysical phenomena. Because the satellite has no propulsion system and atmospheric drag has been continuously lowering its orbit, NASA decided to attempt an orbital boost via a private spacecraft.

Link was built by Katalyst Space Technologies of Arizona. This is the company's first government satellite on-orbit servicing contract. The core challenge of the mission: Swift's orbital altitude over the coming months is uncertain — because atmospheric density varies with the solar activity cycle, directly affecting its orbital decay rate. This means Link must have some autonomous rendezvous and docking capability.

## A New Era for Commercial On-Orbit Servicing

NASA said the mission is "bold and unprecedented." If Link successfully docks with Swift and completes the orbital boost, it will mark the entry of commercial on-orbit servicing technology into practical application. Unlike the traditional model where satellites cannot be repaired after launch, on-orbit servicing can extend the operational life of expensive satellites and reduce the cost of space asset replacement.

## Scientific Value of Swift Observatory

The Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory is one of NASA's most important astronomical observation platforms, primarily tasked with observing gamma-ray bursts, studying active galactic nuclei, and monitoring transient astronomical events. Over 20 years, Swift has provided astronomers worldwide with vast amounts of valuable data, including the discovery of the most distant gamma-ray bursts known.

If successful, Link will perform orbital corrections for Swift, allowing it to continue operating at a more stable altitude for years to come, extending the life of this important scientific platform. The mission also provides technical validation and business model reference for future government satellite life-extension services.
