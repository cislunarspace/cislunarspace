# Shenzhou-23 Crew Harvests First Batch of Space Rice, Multi-Generation Genetic Experiment Advances

**Summary:** On August 2, 2026, CCTV News reported that after more than two months aboard the space station, the Shenzhou-23 crew completed the first batch sample collection and storage for the space rice multi-generation genetic stability experiment.

According to a CCTV News report on August 2, 2026, the Shenzhou-23 astronaut crew successfully completed the first batch sample collection and storage for the space rice multi-generation genetic stability experiment aboard the China Space Station. This marks a significant achievement in space life science research since the crew entered the station over two months ago. The experiment aims to investigate the effects of the microgravity environment on the multi-generation genetic stability of rice.

The report detailed that the astronauts meticulously observed and operated on the rice plants cultivated in orbit, successfully harvesting key samples, including seeds from the first generation of space-grown rice. These valuable samples have been carefully packaged and stored, with plans to return them to Earth on a subsequent re-entry capsule for in-depth analysis by scientists. This harvest signifies the first time the China Space Station has achieved the full life-cycle cultivation of rice from seed to seed in orbit, laying a solid foundation for conducting subsequent multi-generation genetic experiments.

Multi-generation genetic experiments are crucial for understanding the long-term effects of the space environment on plants. By studying the continuous multi-generational growth patterns and genetic variations of rice under unique space conditions such as microgravity and radiation, vital scientific data and technical reserves can be obtained for life support systems in future long-duration deep space exploration missions, particularly for the in-orbit production of food crops. The work carried out by the Shenzhou-23 crew represents another important milestone in space life science research during the application and development phase of the China Space Station.
