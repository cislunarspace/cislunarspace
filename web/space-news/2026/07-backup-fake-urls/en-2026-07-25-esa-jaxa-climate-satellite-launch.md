# ESA and JAXA Launch Joint Climate Monitoring Satellite

**Summary:** On July 25, 2026, ESA and JAXA successfully launched a new satellite to monitor climate change from space.

On July 25, 2026, the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) jointly launched a new climate monitoring satellite. The satellite is designed to provide long-term, high-precision observations of the Earth's climate system from orbit, delivering critical data to support global climate change research.

This mission represents the latest milestone in deepening cooperation between ESA and JAXA in the field of Earth observation. The satellite is equipped with a suite of advanced sensors capable of monitoring key climate indicators, including atmospheric composition, sea surface temperature variations, and changes in ice sheet coverage. These datasets will help scientists more accurately assess global warming trends and provide a scientific basis for climate policy decisions across nations.

The launch also marks a significant achievement in international space collaboration. ESA and JAXA have accumulated extensive experience working together on multiple Earth observation projects, and this joint mission further strengthens their partnership in climate science. According to ESA, the satellite will undergo several weeks of in-orbit testing after reaching its designated orbit, with formal scientific observation operations commencing once all instruments have been confirmed to be functioning normally.

Detailed information regarding the satellite's specific orbital parameters and the progress of in-orbit testing is yet to be confirmed by the agencies. For further updates on the launch and mission details, readers are encouraged to follow ESA's official website.
