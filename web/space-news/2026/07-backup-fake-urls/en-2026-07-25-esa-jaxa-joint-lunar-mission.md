# ESA and JAXA Announce Joint Lunar Science Mission

**Summary:** ESA and JAXA unveiled a collaborative lunar science mission on July 25, 2026, focusing on experiments for future launches.

On July 25, 2026, the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) officially announced a joint lunar science mission. The mission is designed to gather scientific experiment data in support of future crewed and uncrewed lunar exploration programs, marking a significant step in deep-space collaboration between the two agencies.

According to information released by ESA, the joint mission will focus on the integration and validation of multiple scientific experiments. The specific payload manifest and technical specifications are yet to be confirmed by both agencies. Key details such as the mission timeline, launch platform, and launch window have not been disclosed publicly and are expected to be revealed in subsequent official briefings.

In recent years, cooperation between ESA and JAXA in lunar and deep-space exploration has continued to deepen. The two agencies have previously participated in numerous international lunar exploration programs, each bringing distinct expertise in orbiter, lander, and rover technologies. The announcement of this joint mission further underscores a broader trend of international space cooperation moving from conceptual discussions into concrete execution.

Industry analysts note that as the Artemis program and various national lunar exploration initiatives advance, the number of international collaborative missions targeting the Moon is expected to increase significantly in the coming years. This partnership between ESA and JAXA is poised to inject fresh momentum into global lunar science exploration.
