# Artemis II Mission Gets August Target Launch Date

**Summary:** NASA announced on July 25, 2026 that the Artemis II crewed lunar flyby mission is targeting an August launch window from Kennedy Space Center.

NASA officially announced on July 25, 2026 that the highly anticipated Artemis II crewed lunar flyby mission is now targeting an August launch window this year. The mission will lift off from Launch Complex 39B at Kennedy Space Center in Florida, using the Space Launch System (SLS) rocket to send the Orion crew spacecraft around the Moon with four astronauts aboard.

Artemis II represents the first crewed flight to the vicinity of the Moon since Apollo 17 in December 1972. The four crew members, drawn from both American and Canadian astronaut corps, will validate Orion's crewed performance during the lunar flyby, including life support systems, communications, atmospheric re-entry, and other critical operations.

The mission has faced multiple delays in recent years, primarily due to technical issues with the Orion heat shield and various inspection procedures on the SLS rocket. NASA's announcement of the August launch window signals that the mission has entered its final preparation phase. According to reports, the specific launch date within the window will depend on final engineering reviews and weather conditions.

A successful Artemis II flight will pave the way for the subsequent Artemis III mission, which aims to land astronauts near the lunar south pole. The timeline for Artemis III has not yet been finalized and awaits further confirmation from the agency.
